# Card Tricks Curriculum

A progressive syllabus for the one hundred ninety-one tricks in the [Sleight School guide](https://scomofo.github.io/card-tricks/). Foundations first, moves second, routines last — each part builds on the one before it.

---

## Part 1: Foundations

### 1. Key Card Principle
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Secretly track a spectator's card by noting the identity of the card immediately adjacent to it.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Casually note or glimpse the bottom card of the deck before starting (your key card).
  2. Have a card selected, shown to the audience, and returned to the top of the deck.
  3. Cut the deck once, genuinely, about halfway. The cut brings your key card directly on top of the selection. (Do not riffle shuffle here — a genuine shuffle would destroy the setup.)
  4. Spread through the face-up cards. Locate your key card; the spectator's selection is the card immediately below it.
* **Pro Tip:** Never look for the selection directly. Scan smoothly for your key card so your gaze doesn't betray the search.

### 2. Overhand Shuffle Control
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Keep a chosen card exactly where you want it while the shuffle looks honest.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. **To send a card to the bottom:** it starts on top. Shuffle overhand normally — peeling small packets off the top of the right-hand packet into the left hand. The first card peeled (theirs) ends up on the bottom of the reassembled deck. One honest overhand shuffle moves the top card to the bottom.
  2. **To keep a bottom card there:** on later shuffles, leave the bottom stock sitting in your left hand. Lift packets only from the top, peel, and drop them back on top. The bottom cards never move.
  3. **The injog:** to bookmark a position, let the final packet land protruding about a centimeter toward you instead of squared. Cut to that step whenever you need the spot.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the rhythm identical whether you're controlling cards or genuinely mixing them.

### 3. Hindu Shuffle & Force
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Force a predetermined card on a spectator using the rhythm of a Hindu shuffle.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Position your force card on the *top* of the deck.
  2. Hold the deck face-down in the left hand. The right hand comes over the top and pulls the top portion away to the right.
  3. The left thumb strips small packets off the top of the right-hand packet back into the left hand, repeatedly. The right hand keeps hold of the bottom stub throughout.
  4. The bottom card of the right-hand packet never changes during the shuffle — it is always the original top card, your force card. Have the spectator say "stop" at any point, then tilt the right-hand packet to display its bottom card as their "free choice."
  5. Drop the stub on top to finish, holding a left pinky break where it lands so you can cut straight to the force card later.
* **Pro Tip:** Don't pause when they say stop — flow straight into turning the packet over. Hesitation is what makes a force look like a force.

### 4. Double Lift
* **Difficulty:** Beginner / Intermediate
* **Objective:** Lift two cards as a single card — the engine inside half of card magic.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. As you square the deck, hold a small break with the left pinky under the top two cards.
  2. The right hand takes the double from above by the ends — thumb at the inner end, fingers at the outer — lifting both cards as one.
  3. Turn it over like a book page to display the face, then turn it back the same way.
  4. The top card of the deck is now secretly the second card.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the two cards perfectly squared. A split edge is the only tell — practice the turnover in a mirror until a double feels identical to a single.

### 5. False Cut
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Appear to cut the deck while leaving the entire order undisturbed.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. With the deck in the right hand, the left hand swing-cuts the top half off.
  2. Complete the cut: place the right-hand half on top of the left-hand half. One genuine cut — the halves have swapped.
  3. Do the exact same thing a second time. Two identical half-cuts restore the original order completely.
* **Pro Tip:** Two cuts look more honest than one. Do it mid-sentence, casually, exactly as you would before any trick.

### 6. Classic Force
* **Difficulty:** Beginner / Intermediate
* **Objective:** Force a predetermined card with nothing but timing — no shuffle, no setup.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Place the force card on top of the deck.
  2. Hold the deck face-down and tell the spectator you'll riffle through the cards; they should just touch the back of one.
  3. Riffle down the outer edge, extending the deck toward their hand. Watch their hand, not the cards.
  4. Slow the riffle so the instant their finger commits, the card beneath it is the top card — your force. Stop the moment they touch.
* **Pro Tip:** This runs on timing, not mechanics, which is why it looks so fair. When it misses, smile and move on — a shrugged-off miss is invisible.

### 7. The Palm
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Secretly steal the top card into your palm — the first real vanish.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. The card to vanish starts on top, deck in left-hand dealing grip.
  2. The right hand comes over to square the deck.
  3. As it lifts away, it presses down gently and carries the top card with it, the card bowing slightly into the palm under finger pressure.
  4. Keep the hand moving and natural — fingers softly curled, never flat and frozen.
  5. Reverse the action under cover of squaring the deck to replace the card.
* **Pro Tip:** Palming is ninety percent acting. Flash yourself in the mirror on purpose to learn the bad angles.

### 8. The Peek
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Learn a card's identity without anyone knowing you looked.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. **Bottom peek:** tilt the deck a fraction toward yourself while gesturing and glimpse the bottom index. One glance — staring gets caught.
  2. **Top peek:** in dealing grip, the left thumb buckles the top card just enough to sight its index, covered by tapping the deck square.
  3. **Riffle peek:** riffle up the back of the deck, lifting the top card just high enough to sight it.
* **Pro Tip:** Your eyes work while your mouth keeps talking. Nobody watches a person who's mid-sentence.

### 9. Charlier Cut
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Cut the deck one-handed — pure flourish, no secret, all style.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Deck at the left fingertips: thumb on one long edge, middle and ring fingers on the other.
  2. Let the bottom half drop into the palm, pivoting against the thumb.
  3. The index finger pushes the top half forward; it swings down onto the bottom half. Halves swapped, one-handed.
* **Pro Tip:** Flourishes are the salt of card magic — a pinch improves everything.

### 10. The Break (Pinky Break)
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Maintain a secret separation between sections of the deck using minimal hand tension.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold the deck in dealing grip. As a card drops onto the deck or is handled, allow the pad of the left pinky finger to catch a tiny corner or edge of the separating card.
  2. Keep the flesh of the pinky soft; a deep, rigid intrusion creates a visible gap on the sides of the deck.
  3. Use the break as a temporary holding station for controls, double lifts, or passes.
* **Pro Tip:** Practice squaring the deck completely from the front and sides while your left pinky maintains the separation invisibly from the rear.
* **On the site:** Card t65, "The Pinky Break."

### 11. The Spread Cull
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Secretly extract or control target cards to the top or bottom of the deck while spreading face-up.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Spread through the cards face-up under the guise of searching for a card or showing the mix.
  2. When reaching the target card, ride it beneath the spread using the right fingers under cover of the top cards.
  3. Draw the card flush underneath to the top or bottom position as you square the spread.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the rhythm of the spread completely uniform; pausing or jerking when reaching the card exposes the cull.

### 12. Swing Cut
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Cut the deck with a natural swinging action — also the engine inside many false cuts.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold the deck in the right hand from above (Biddle grip).
  2. The left hand swings in and takes roughly the top half — thumb on the face side, fingers on the back.
  3. Complete the cut by placing the right-hand packet on top, or leave the packets separated to continue into a false sequence.
  4. Match the rhythm of a genuine cut whether the order is preserved or not.
* **Pro Tip:** A swing cut into a brief out-jog, followed by a square-up that leaves the original top stock in place, is a workhorse false cut.
* **On the site:** Card t66, "Swing Cut."

### 13. Breather Crimp
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** A subtle permanent bend that lets you cut to a card by feel alone.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Soft lengthwise or widthwise bend under casual handling.
  2. Survives shuffles; invisible at a glance.
  3. Riffle or cut — the deck breaks at the crimp.
* **Pro Tip:** Less bend is more. If you can see it, they can see it.
* **On the site:** Card t82, "Breather Crimp."

### 14. Injog Shuffle Control
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Bookmark a card during an overhand shuffle with a protruding injog.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Selection or target stock on top.
  2. During the shuffle, let the key packet land injogged toward you.
  3. Later cut to the injog or undercut it to complete the control.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the rest of the shuffle square. Only one packet should step out.
* **On the site:** Card t83, "Injog Shuffle Control."

## Part 2: The Moves

### 1. The Glide
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Secretly deal the second-from-bottom card while appearing to deal the bottom card.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold the deck face-down in the left hand.
  2. The left fingertips pull the bottom card back about half an inch toward the palm, exposing the card directly above it.
  3. The right hand takes the exposed card — the second from the bottom — leaving the original bottom card concealed behind in the left hand.
* **Pro Tip:** Never tilt the face of the deck toward the audience while the bottom card is pulled back. The audience must believe they just saw the bottom card dealt.

### 2. Riffle Force
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Force a predetermined card while the spectator believes their "stop" chose it.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Place the force card on top of the deck.
  2. Hold the deck in the left hand and riffle down the outer edge with the right thumb. The spectator says "stop" whenever they like.
  3. Freeze at the break. The right hand lifts the upper packet at the stop point — and as it lifts, the left thumb secretly drags the top card back so it stays on the lower packet. Keep the motion small and covered by the right hand.
  4. Display the top card of the lower packet: "the card you stopped at."
* **Pro Tip:** Protect the spectator's belief that the stop chose the card. That belief does most of the work.

### 3. Elmsley Count
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Count four cards aloud while one of them never shows its face.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold four cards face-down in the left hand.
  2. With the right hand, take the cards one at a time off the top, counting "1, 2, 3, 4."
  3. Cards 2 and 4 are taken *under* the right-hand packet instead of on top — the right hand dips slightly to receive each one beneath.
  4. The audience sees four cards counted; one card was hidden the entire time.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the rhythm metronome-even. Rushing counts 2 and 4 is what exposes the dip — slow is smooth, and smooth is invisible.

### 4. Snap Change
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate / Advanced
* **Objective:** Visibly transform one face-up card into another with a snap of the fingers.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold a small packet face-up in the left hand. The right hand comes over from above.
  2. Press the right middle and ring fingertips onto the face of the top card, near its top edge.
  3. In one sharp snapping motion, drag the top card back toward you — it flips face-down under cover of the right hand, revealing the card beneath. The snap sound plus the speed is what reads as a visual change.
  4. Commit fully to the motion; hesitation is the only thing that exposes it.
* **Pro Tip:** Play it to someone directly in front of you with the packet held low. It's devastating head-on and ordinary from the side.

### 5. Top Change
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Switch one card for another in the half-second between your hands.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. The known card sits on top of the deck (left hand); the switch card is face-down in the right hand.
  2. Bring the right hand over the deck as if laying its card on top.
  3. In one passing motion the right hand drops its card while the left thumb slides the original top card into the right hand. Neither hand stops.
  4. The right hand continues on, carrying the stolen card away naturally.
* **Pro Tip:** Speed is not the point — the logic of the gesture is the cover. If the gesture makes sense, nobody watches the cards.

### 6. The Pass
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Transpose the deck's halves invisibly — the classic secret cut.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Left hand holds the deck with a pinky break between the halves.
  2. Right hand covers the deck, thumb inner end, fingers outer.
  3. The hands pivot: the bottom half swings up and over as the top half drops beneath.
  4. Square up immediately, the way you would after any ordinary cut.
* **Pro Tip:** Graduate school — months, not days. Until it's invisible, the overhand control does the same job honestly.

### 7. The Jordan Count
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Count four cards while hiding the identity or orientation of a specific card (typically the third card).
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold four cards face-down in the left hand.
  2. Count the first card individually into the right hand.
  3. As you take the second card on top of the first, turn both cards slightly or execute a clean transfer so that when you take the third card, you recount or redisplay elements, concealing the key card face.
  4. Finish by taking the fourth card cleanly on top.
* **Pro Tip:** Like the Elmsley count, focus entirely on the rhythm of your voice and eyes rather than looking down at your fingers during the transfer steps.
* **On the site:** Card t67, "Jordan Count."

### 8. The Side Steal
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Secretly draw a chosen card sideways out of the deck into a palm or directly to the top.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Maintain a pinky break above the chosen card in the center of the deck.
  2. Cover the deck with the right hand as if squaring it.
  3. Use the right fingertips to push the card out sideways into the right palm, shifting it to the top as the hands separate.
* **Pro Tip:** Perform the steal during a natural square-up or gesture, moving the body rather than isolating the hands.

### 9. Marlo Tilt
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Create the convincing optical illusion of inserting a selection into the middle when it goes second from the top.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Obtain a subtle pinky break under the top card at the rear of the deck.
  2. Tilt the front edge of the deck down toward the spectator while slightly lifting the rear edge of the top card.
  3. Slide the selected card into the rear gap — from the front angle, it appears to slide directly into the deck's equator.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the tilt angle low and subtle; over-tilting reveals the gap behind the top card.
* **On the site:** Card t51, "The Tilt."

### 10. Erdnase Color Change
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate / Advanced
* **Objective:** Visibly transform the face of a card by wiping the palm flat across the deck.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold the deck face-up in a dealing grip.
  2. Sweep the right palm flat across the face card, using the heel of the hand to secretly drag the second card from top forward.
  3. Draw the second card over the face card as the palm clears, wiping down to reveal the transformation.
* **Pro Tip:** Let the motion be a smooth, continuous wave rather than an abrupt snatching movement.
* **On the site:** Card t50, "Erdnase Color Change."

### 11. Second Deal
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Deal the second card while appearing to deal the top — the foundation of false dealing and gambling demos.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. The top card is the one that must stay; the second card is the one that leaves.
  2. Left thumb pushes two cards as a unit, then draws the top card back as the right hand takes the exposed second.
  3. Match the timing, wrist turn, and sound of a normal deal.
  4. Strike seconds (from a squared deck) and push-off seconds are both worth learning.
* **Pro Tip:** Start painfully slow in a mirror. Speed only after the dual-card push is invisible at half tempo.
* **On the site:** Card t68, "Second Deal."

### 12. Ascanio Spread
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Spread a packet to show fewer cards than you hold by keeping a secret block squared as one.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold a small packet (often five cards to be shown as four).
  2. Spread between the hands while keeping two cards perfectly aligned as a single unit.
  3. Display the visible faces; the extra card rides hidden in the block.
  4. Resquare casually — the count remains secret.
* **Pro Tip:** It should look like a soft display, not a move. Even light on the faces sells the illusion.
* **On the site:** Card t69, "Ascanio Spread."

### 13. Bottom Deal
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Deal the bottom card while appearing to deal from the top.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Bottom card is the one that will leave.
  2. Right hand approaches as for a normal top deal.
  3. Left fingers buckle or free the bottom card into the right hand.
  4. Match the rhythm and wrist turn of an honest deal.
* **Pro Tip:** Angle the front of the deck slightly down so the bottom edge stays hidden. Soft deck, slow practice.
* **On the site:** Card t75, "Bottom Deal."

### 14. Triple Lift
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Lift three cards as one — extends the double lift for switches and ambitious phases.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Pinky break under the top three cards.
  2. Right hand lifts all three from above, edges squared.
  3. Book-style turnover to display, then replace.
  4. Top card is now third from the top.
* **Pro Tip:** If a double is hard to keep square, a triple is harder. Drill until three feels like one.
* **On the site:** Card t76, "Triple Lift."

### 15. Hamman Count
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Count a packet while switching the two halves mid-count to hide cards or faces.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Begin counting from left hand into right.
  2. Mid-count, exchange stocks so the original left packet is recounted under a new face.
  3. Finish the spoken count — number matches, faces lie.
* **Pro Tip:** The switch must live inside the beat of the count. Steady tempo; do not look at your hands.
* **On the site:** Card t77, "Hamman Count."

### 16. Bottom Palm
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Steal the bottom card into the left palm while handing the deck away.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Bottom card targeted.
  2. Right hand takes the deck; left fingers curl the bottom card into the palm.
  3. Deck leaves; card stays for later production or load.
* **Pro Tip:** Same acting rules as the top palm — soft hand, natural motion, never freeze.
* **On the site:** Card t81, "Bottom Palm."

### 17. Flick Change
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Visually change a face-up card with a sharp flick or twirl.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Face-up card in view; second card hidden in grip.
  2. Sharp flick exchanges them in a blur.
  3. Hold for a beat; ditch the extra on the offbeat.
* **Pro Tip:** Commit fully. Hesitation turns a spark into a move.
* **On the site:** Card t84, "Flick Change."

### 18. One-Hand Top Palm
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Steal the top card into the right palm with one hand.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Deck in right hand; top card targeted.
  2. Fingers and heel lever the card into the palm during a square or gesture.
  3. Left hand free to take or hand off the deck.
* **Pro Tip:** Practice until the palm looks like a casual square.
* **On the site:** Card t89, "One-Hand Top Palm."

### 19. Mercury Card Fold
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Fold a card into quarters in the palm for impossible loads.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Card in palm; fold lengthwise, then widthwise.
  2. Compact packet stays hidden in the hand.
  3. Load into pocket, wallet, or envelope.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the hand soft. A tight fist telegraphs the fold.
* **On the site:** Card t90, "Mercury Card Fold."

### 20. False Riffle Shuffle
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Riffle that looks thorough but preserves order or a large stock.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Split and riffle so one half strips back or a Zarrow-style action preserves order.
  2. Square as if mixed.
* **Pro Tip:** Match the sound and body language of a real riffle.
* **On the site:** Card t91, "False Riffle Shuffle."

### 21. Center Deal
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Deal from the middle while appearing to deal the top.
* **Mechanics:** Break or step marks the card; deal takes from the step; top stock stays.
* **Pro Tip:** Master second and bottom deals first. Angle-sensitive.
* **On the site:** Card t97, "Center Deal."

### 22. Greek Deal
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Deal the second-from-bottom while appearing to deal the top.
* **Mechanics:** Bottom stays; the card above it is taken. Extreme angle care.
* **Pro Tip:** Mirror work at half speed. If you see the bottom edge, so do they.
* **On the site:** Card t98, "Greek Deal."

### 23. Overhand Stack
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Build pairs, trips, or a full house during an overhand shuffle.
* **Mechanics:** Run cards singly or in groups so targets land at deal positions.
* **Pro Tip:** Start with a pair. Add complexity only after the run-up is invisible.
* **On the site:** Card t99, "Overhand Stack."

### 24. The Hop
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Restore the top stock after the spectator cuts on the table.
* **Mechanics:** As you complete their cut, hop the original top half back on top under the square-up.
* **Pro Tip:** The hop is a sound and a blur — the square-up sells honesty.
* **On the site:** Card t100, "The Hop."

### 25. The Cooler
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Switch the entire deck for a stacked cold deck.
* **Mechanics:** Hot in play; cold ready; switch under case, square-up, or body turn.
* **Pro Tip:** Misdirection first, mechanics second.
* **On the site:** Card t101, "The Cooler."

### 26. Herrmann Pass
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Quiet covered pass — halves transpose under the right hand.
* **Mechanics:** Pinky break; right hand covers; bottom half swings over as top drops under; square soft.
* **Pro Tip:** Often more angle-friendly than a classic pass. Match cover to your hands.
* **On the site:** Card t107, "Herrmann Pass."

### 27. Half Pass
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Secretly reverse the bottom half under cover of the top stock.
* **Mechanics:** Right hand screens; left fingers flip the bottom section; square. Ready for Triumph and reverses.
* **Pro Tip:** Top cards must not move. All motion stays below the cover.
* **On the site:** Card t108, "Half Pass."

### 28. Braue Reversal
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Reverse a card while cutting the deck.
* **Mechanics:** Cut half to the right; selection flips against the left packet as the cut completes.
* **Pro Tip:** The cut is the move. Don't pause on the flip.
* **On the site:** Card t109, "Braue Reversal."

### 29. Bottom Change
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Switch a right-hand card for the bottom card of the deck in a pass.
* **Mechanics:** Right hand leaves its card and takes the bottom card in one continuous gesture.
* **Pro Tip:** Same rule as the top change — the gesture must make sense.
* **On the site:** Card t110, "Bottom Change."

### 30. Flushtration Count
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Show multiple cards as identical by repeatedly displaying the same bottom card.
* **Mechanics:** Show bottom face-up; turn down; deal one; repeat. Audience believes every card matched.
* **Pro Tip:** Rhythm and confidence sell it.
* **On the site:** Card t111, "Flushtration Count."

### 31. Gambler's Cop
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Low natural palm — card in curled fingers, hand looks relaxed and empty.
* **Mechanics:** Card settles into finger curl and heel; hand hangs at the side; produce later.
* **Pro Tip:** Relaxation is the camouflage. A stiff hand announces the steal.
* **On the site:** Card t112, "Gambler's Cop."

### 32. Tenkai Palm
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Card held flat against the palm by the thumb — open-looking vanish/transfer position.
* **Mechanics:** Thumb pins card flat on upper palm; fingers free; produce or transfer as needed.
* **Pro Tip:** Soft thumb. A hard pinch flashes the edge.
* **On the site:** Card t113, "Tenkai Palm."

### 33. Through-the-Fist Change
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Push a card through the fist; it emerges face-down or transformed.
* **Mechanics:** Card into top of fist; hidden turn inside; emerges changed from the bottom.
* **Pro Tip:** Don't rush the emergence — the change needs a beat.
* **On the site:** Card t114, "Through-the-Fist Change."

### 34. Spread Pass
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Pass the halves while the deck is spread between the hands.
* **Mechanics:** Spread opens; break is passed under the moving cards; resquare as a search or display.
* **Pro Tip:** The spread must look motivated — searching for a card works well.
* **On the site:** Card t115, "Spread Pass."

### 35. Diagonal Palm Shift
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Steal a center card into the palm as the deck is pushed flush (Erdnase).
* **Mechanics:** Card angled; as it goes flush it slides into the right palm; deck squared and fair.
* **Pro Tip:** One continuous push. Any pause telegraphs the steal.
* **On the site:** Card t116, "Diagonal Palm Shift."

### 36. Midnight Shift
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Pass the halves as the deck is turned over.
* **Mechanics:** Break held; deck turns face-up/down; halves transpose under the larger motion.
* **Pro Tip:** Motivate the turnover with a face-up display or gesture.
* **On the site:** Card t117, "Midnight Shift."

### 37. Cover Pass
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Pass under cover of a right-hand packet held above the deck.
* **Mechanics:** Cover packet held still; lower halves pass; cover replaced. Nothing looked like a cut.
* **Pro Tip:** Cover packet must look intentional — display or count.
* **On the site:** Card t118, "Cover Pass."

### 38. Multiple Shift
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Control several selections to top or bottom in one shift.
* **Mechanics:** Insert at different depths; one shift gathers them all.
* **Pro Tip:** Clean insertions matter more than a fast shift.
* **On the site:** Card t119, "Multiple Shift."

### 39. Buckle Count
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Count a packet while hiding cards via a bottom-card buckle.
* **Mechanics:** Buckle bottom card; take block above as one; finish spoken count.
* **Pro Tip:** Soft buckle — a loud snap gives it away.
* **On the site:** Card t120, "Buckle Count."

### 40. Pinky Count
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Secretly count cards with the left pinky to a precise break.
* **Mechanics:** Pinky pulls down one card at a time at the rear; front stays square.
* **Pro Tip:** Start with three or four. Speed and silence come with repetition.
* **On the site:** Card t121, "Pinky Count."

### 41. Lateral Palm
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Card clipped at the hand's edge — nearly invisible head-on.
* **Mechanics:** Clip between thumb base and mid-fingers; flat against the edge; produce or transfer.
* **Pro Tip:** Angle-specific. Rehearse working angles before using live.
* **On the site:** Card t122, "Lateral Palm."

### 42. Downs Change
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Visual change via a covered slide or wipe.
* **Mechanics:** Face-up display; wipe slides face away and leaves the card beneath.

## Part 3: Routines

### 1. 21-Card Trick
* **Difficulty:** Beginner (Self-Working)
* **Objective:** A mathematical classic — the spectator's card isolates itself at position 11 with zero sleight of hand.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Deal 21 cards face-up into three columns of seven, dealing across row by row.
  2. The spectator memorizes a card and tells you which column it's in.
  3. Gather the columns so their column ends up sandwiched *between* the other two.
  4. Repeat the deal-and-gather twice more — three rounds total, their column in the middle every time.
  5. Their card is now exactly 11th from the top. Count down and reveal it.
* **Pro Tip:** Each round narrows the card's possible position; three rounds leave exactly one. Keep casual conversation going while gathering so nobody tracks the pattern.

### 2. Two-Card Monte
* **Difficulty:** Beginner / Intermediate
* **Objective:** A pocket-sized impossibility — the spectator can never find the Ace, no matter how closely they watch.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Show two cards, e.g. the Ace of Spades and the King of Hearts. "Watch the Ace."
  2. Turn the two-card packet face-down *as one block*, exactly like a double lift. The order never changes — that is the entire method.
  3. Do the "throw": toss or flip the packet between your hands with a flourish. It looks like a switch. It isn't.
  4. "Where's the Ace — top or bottom?" Whatever they answer, show them they're wrong.
  5. Repeat. The comedy is that they can never win — lean into it.
* **Pro Tip:** Nothing sneaky happened, yet the spectator is certain something did. Conviction beats complexity; sell the game, not the method.

### 3. Ambitious Card
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A signed card repeatedly rises from the middle of the deck back to the top.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. A card is selected, signed, and controlled to the top.
  2. Push it face-up into the middle of the deck. Snap your fingers — it's back on top.
  3. Show it with a double lift, turn the double face-down, then take the top card (secretly an indifferent card) and insert *that* into the middle. The spectator swears their card went in; it never left the top.
  4. Repeat with escalating impossibility — lose it for real, box the deck — until the finale.
* **Pro Tip:** Vary the control method in each phase so the trick can't be reverse-engineered. Two clean phases beat four shaky ones; add a phase a week and the routine grows with you.

### 4. Triumph
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A deck shuffled face-up into face-down rights itself completely — except for the spectator's card.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. A card is chosen, signed, and controlled to the top, placed face-up on the face-down deck.
  2. Protect that card's orientation while apparently mixing the deck: overhand shuffles that keep the top stock, false cuts — turning a few packets face-up as you go. The mixing is theater; the one orientation you protect is the trick.
  3. Square the deck slowly and spread it: every card face-down except theirs, face-up.
* **Pro Tip:** The classic Vernon handling uses a slip shuffle for the mixing phase. Whatever handling you choose, the shuffle must be false or controlled — a genuinely randomized deck can't be unmixed. The spectator watched the mess happen, which is why the cleanup lands so hard.
### 5. Oil and Water
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Reds and blacks, mixed together, separate themselves — the classic Elmsley-count routine.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Show three red cards, then three black cards.
  2. Mix them face-down alternately, then gather the packet so the colors quietly regroup.
  3. Elmsley count the packet, showing "mixed" reds and blacks — the count hides the grouping.
  4. With a snap, deal two piles: all reds, all blacks.
* **Pro Tip:** The audience remembers the mixing and the separation. Nobody remembers the gathering — which is where the trick lives.

### 6. Twisting the Aces
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate / Advanced
* **Objective:** Four face-down aces turn face-up one by one with each twist of the packet (Dai Vernon).
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Four aces face-down, in Clubs-Hearts-Spades-Diamonds order from the top.
  2. Triple turnover to show the Ace of Spades ("the heaviest ace"), then turn face-down.
  3. Twist the packet 180 degrees; Elmsley count shows the Ace of Hearts face-up.
  4. Twist again; Elmsley count with the last card underneath shows the Ace of Clubs.
  5. The Ace of Diamonds resists — then turns face-up in the middle on the final twist.
* **Pro Tip:** Vernon invented the twisting motion to justify the Elmsley grip. They watch the twist, never the count.

### 7. Chicago Opener
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A selected card changes its back color, then transforms into a second selection (Al Leech).
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Setup: an odd-backed duplicate on top of the deck; force its mate (the Hindu force works well).
  2. Control the selection to the top; double lift to show its back has turned red.
  3. Deal the red-backed card to the table; the original is secretly back on top.
  4. A second card is selected and controlled to the top.
  5. Pick up the tabled card and switch it for the top card (the top change earns its keep); reveal it as the second selection.
* **Pro Tip:** Two miracles, one odd-backed card. Requires a duplicate with a different-colored back.
### 8. Biddle Trick
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A selected card vanishes from among five cards and appears somewhere impossible (Elmer Biddle).
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Card selected and controlled to the top; deal the top five into a packet.
  2. Spread them — the spectator confirms their card is there.
  3. Count the packet face-up; on the second card, execute the Biddle steal, keeping it back under the packet.
  4. Their card has vanished from the five — reveal it face-up in the deck or from your pocket.
* **Pro Tip:** The steal happens while they're answering your question. Questions are misdirection you never choreograph.

### 9. Do As I Do
* **Difficulty:** Beginner (Self-Working)
* **Objective:** You and the spectator each follow the same steps with your own halves — and find the same card.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Spectator cuts into two piles; you each take one and shuffle.
  2. Each picks a card to remember — but you only glimpse the bottom card of your pile (your key); your "selection" is theater.
  3. Each places their card on their pile; your pile goes on top of theirs, putting your key directly above their selection.
  4. They cut the combined deck. You find your key — the card beneath it is theirs. Reveal together: they match.
* **Pro Tip:** The "do as I do" patter keeps their eyes on their own hands.

### 10. Card to Pocket
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A signed card leaves the deck and lands in your pocket — the palm's graduation piece.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Card selected, signed, controlled to the top.
  2. Palm it under cover of squaring the deck.
  3. Hand them the deck; produce the signed card from your (or their) pocket.
* **Pro Tip:** The trick isn't the palm — it's the ordinary moments around it.

### 11. Cannibal Cards
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Three spectator-selected "missionary" cards are fed to four kings, only to vanish and reappear inside the kings' stomachs.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Display the four Kings (the "cannibals") and have three spectators choose cards (the "missionaries").
  2. Place one missionary card under each of the first three kings, and tuck the fourth king on top or bottom.
  3. Perform a magical gesture or brief narrative beat illustrating how the kings eat the missionaries.
  4. Spread the four kings to reveal that three cards are now trapped between them, turning out to be the three chosen signatures.
* **Pro Tip:** The narrative framing carries this routine; pace the story so the audience visualizes each "meal" before the final spread.

### 12. The Gathering of the Clan (Aces Routine)
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Four aces placed into different parts of the deck magically travel to assemble together in one packet.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Show the four aces clearly, then deal them face-down into four separate packets on the table, burying each under three indifferent cards.
  2. Perform a magical gesture over each packet in turn.
  3. Turn over the first three packets to show the aces have vanished.
  4. Turn over the final packet to reveal all four aces gathered together.
* **Pro Tip:** Use subtle packet switches or a classic master move (like the Stanyon or McDonald handling) to establish the premise cleanly before the magic happens.

### 13. Paul Harris' Reset
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate / Advanced
* **Objective:** Transpose four Aces and four Kings one by one, then instantly reset both packets.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Display four Aces in hand and four Kings on the table.
  2. Using Elmsley counts and pinky breaks, transform each Ace into a King sequentially, placing transformed cards onto the tabled pile.
  3. Perform a magical gesture over both packets.
  4. Instantly spread both sets to reveal the Aces have returned to your hand and the Kings to the table.
* **Pro Tip:** The instant "reset" finish dismantling card count tracking is what makes the effect so fooling.

### 14. Stewart James' "Further Than That"
* **Difficulty:** Beginner (Self-Working Stack)
* **Objective:** A multi-phase self-working masterwork featuring a forced selection, spelled revelation, Ace assembly, and Royal Flush kicker.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Pre-arrange a 14-card top stack (Spades 2–7, 3 non-Spade Aces, Ace of Spades at 10th, Spades 10–K at 11–14).
  2. Spectator names a number from 10 to 20 (e.g. 13); deal 13 cards, add the digits (1 + 3 = 4), and count down to force the Ace of Spades.
  3. Spell "A-C-E O-F S-P-A-D-E-S" to produce the selection.
  4. Turn over the tops of the dealt packets to reveal the other three Aces.
  5. Reveal the remaining stack cards to complete a Royal Flush in Spades.
* **Pro Tip:** Deliver the phrase "further than that" between phases to anchor the escalating visual rewards.

### 15. John Bannon's Play It Straight Triumph
* **Difficulty:** Beginner / Intermediate (Self-Working)
* **Objective:** A self-working Triumph where face-up and face-down shuffles unmix to reveal an entire suit arranged numerically, except for the selection.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Have a spectator select and remember a card.
  2. Interleave face-up and face-down packets according to Bannon's procedural system.
  3. Ribbon-spread the deck: every card has turned face-down except for 12 cards of the chosen suit in Ace-to-King order, with only their selection missing.
* **Pro Tip:** Frame this as a demonstration of mental order over chaos rather than manual dexterity.

### 16. Dr. Daley's Last Trick
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Two aces change places under conditions that look completely fair.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Show a red ace and a black ace clearly.
  2. Place each face-down under a hand or at opposite corners of the mat.
  3. The work is in the placements — double lifts or top changes switch the identities.
  4. Reveal: they have transposed.
* **Pro Tip:** Conviction about which ace is where is the whole trick. Clean displays beat fancy switches.
* **On the site:** Card t70, "Dr. Daley's Last Trick."

### 17. Homing Card
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A selected card keeps returning to the hand no matter how often it is lost in the deck.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Selection signed and controlled to the top.
  2. Lose it fairly in the middle; immediately control it back.
  3. Produce it from the hand or show it back on top.
  4. Repeat with escalating controls (undercut, tilt, pass). End clean.
* **Pro Tip:** Vary the control each phase so the method never settles into a readable pattern. Natural sequel to Ambitious Card.
* **On the site:** Card t71, "Homing Card."

### 18. Card Warp
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A folded card turns inside out while trapped inside another folded card — pure visual impossibility.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Fold two cards in half (cover card and warp card).
  2. Slide the warp card partway into the folded cover.
  3. Push it through; a hidden orientation change turns it inside out while it stays caged.
  4. Display the reversed card still trapped in the fold.
* **Pro Tip:** The original Roy Walton handling remains the clearest. Go slow on the push so the visual registers.
* **On the site:** Card t72, "Card Warp."

### 19. Between Your Palms
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Their signed card appears between two cards they hold — or between their own palms.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Selection signed and brought to a stealable position.
  2. Two indifferent cards (or empty hands) are shown and held by the spectator.
  3. Load the signed card between them under cover of a gesture or the deck.
  4. They open the sandwich / their hands — signed card in the middle.
* **Pro Tip:** The stronger the conviction their hands were empty, the harder the appearance hits. Don't rush the clean display.
* **On the site:** Card t73, "Between Your Palms."

### 20. Invisible Palm
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Visually vanish a card into an apparently empty hand, then reproduce it at will.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Display the card at the fingertips.
  2. Close the hand or push the card through the fist — hand comes away empty.
  3. Casually show the hand empty.
  4. Produce from the same hand, pocket, or deck.
* **Pro Tip:** One clean vanish and one strong reproduction beat three muddy ones.
* **On the site:** Card t79, "Invisible Palm."

### 21. Everywhere and Nowhere
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate

* **Routine Flow:**
  1. False shows at top, middle, bottom (doubles/switches).
  2. Vanish from the deck.
  3. Produce from pocket, mouth, envelope, or wallet.
* **Pro Tip:** Sell every display. The vanish only hurts if they believed the earlier shows.
* **On the site:** Card t85, "Everywhere and Nowhere."

### 22. Elevator Cards

### 23. Color-Changing Deck

### 24. Card to Wallet

### 25. Card Under Glass

### 26. Full-Deck Brainwave

### 28. Tossed-Out Deck

### 29. Think of a Card

## Part 4: Systems & Self-Workers

### 26. Si Stebbins Stack
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Memorize a full-deck stack that survives any number of cuts, enabling apparent mind-reading.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Arrange the deck so suits cycle Clubs, Hearts, Spades, Diamonds ("CHaSeD") and each value is 3 higher than the previous card (A=1 … K=13, wrapping): A♣, 4♥, 7♠, 10♦, K♣, 3♥, and so on through all 52.
  2. The sequence is a closed cycle, so the deck may be cut any number of times without breaking the order.
  3. Glimpse any single card (the bottom is easiest). Add 3 to its value (wrapping past King to Ace) and step its suit one place forward in CHaSeD order to name the card below it; subtract and step back to name the card above it.
* **Pro Tip:** Drill the stack with the deck in your hands — deal and name each next card — until the +3/CHaSeD step is instant.

### 27. Out of This World
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** The spectator separates reds from blacks without looking, while you never touch the cards.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Prearrange: all 26 reds on top of the deck, all 26 blacks beneath. Cover with casual shuffling patter.
  2. The spectator deals the cards face-down into two piles, trusting their gut about which pile each card belongs to.
  3. Insist they finish one pile before starting the other ("deal about half here, then the rest here"). Since the first 26 cards are all red and the last 26 all black, the piles separate themselves.
  4. Spread both piles to show one all red, one all black.
* **Pro Tip:** Never touch the deck after the setup — the trick's power comes from your visible lack of involvement.

### 28. Fitch Cheney's Five-Card Trick
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** With a partner out of the room, encode a hidden card's identity in the arrangement of four visible cards.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. The spectator selects any five cards; the partner leaves the room.
  2. By the pigeonhole principle, two of the five share a suit. Hide one of that pair and place its mate face-up first — it silently communicates the suit.
  3. Rank the remaining three cards low/medium/high. Their six possible left-to-right orderings encode the numbers 1–6.
  4. Counting clockwise through the suit's values, the hidden card always lies within 6 steps ahead of the suit card, so the ordering tells the partner exactly how far to count forward.
  5. The partner returns, reads the suit from the first card and the number from the order of the other three, and names the hidden card.
* **Pro Tip:** Agree the ordering code (e.g., low-medium-high = 1, low-high-medium = 2…) in advance and drill it until it's automatic.

### 29. Ten-Card Poker Deal
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Win an "unbeatable" poker hand that the spectator deals themselves.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Pre-stack ten cards so a strict alternating deal gives you a straight flush and the spectator four of a kind. Classic shape, top to bottom: K♣, 5♥, K♦, 6♥, K♥, 7♥, K♠, 8♥, Q♣, 9♥.
  2. Deal the first two cards yourself, one to each hand — this locks who receives the odd and even positions.
  3. Let the spectator deal the rest, even face-up. The fixed order means the hands were decided before the deal began.
  4. They turn over four kings; you turn over the 5♥–9♥ straight flush.
* **Pro Tip:** Many published stacks exist (Buckley, Lorayne, Trost). Pick one and never vary it — hesitation during the deal is what spectators remember.

### 30. The Clock Trick
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** A named hour always matches the card dealt to that clock position.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Secretly arrange thirteen cards, Ace through King in order, on top of the deck.
  2. Have the spectator deal twelve of them face-down in a circle like clock hours, starting at 1 o'clock. Keep the thirteenth card (the King) in your hand.
  3. Ask for any hour from 1 to 12 and flip the card at that position — position N received the Nth card of the Ace-to-Queen sequence, so it always matches.
  4. Reveal the King as the thirteenth card "watching over" all twelve hours.
* **Pro Tip:** Let them deal slowly and name the hour before you touch anything — the less you handle the cards, the stronger it plays.

### 31. Miraskill
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Predict, twice, the outcome of a pair-separation the spectator performs with a shuffled deck.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Beforehand, secretly remove and pocket four red cards. The spectator genuinely shuffles the remaining 48.
  2. Write a prediction ("your pile will come up four cards short") and set it face-down in view.
  3. The spectator deals the deck into face-up pairs: red-red pairs pile in front of them, black-black pairs in front of you, mixed pairs discarded.
  4. Mixed pairs consume equal reds and blacks, so the piles would always come out even — except you stole four reds, so their pile lands exactly four short, as predicted.
  5. Under cover of the counting, slip the four cards back into the discards; gather everything and predict — correctly — that both piles match on the second round.
* **Pro Tip:** The prediction must be on paper before the dealing starts. A prediction made after the fact is just a observation.

### 32. Cross Cut Force
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Force a card using the spectator's own cut and a crosswise "marker."
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Place the force card on top of the deck and set the deck on the table.
  2. The spectator cuts: they lift off the top portion and set it beside the rest.
  3. Take the remaining bottom portion and lay it crosswise on top of their packet, "marking the exact spot they cut to."
  4. Chat for a beat so the cross reads as a placeholder, then lift the crosswise packet away.
  5. They take the top card of the lower packet — the original top card of the deck, your force.
* **Pro Tip:** The casual chat between marking and revealing is the whole trick. Rush it and the cross looks like a move; linger and it looks like nothing.

### 33. Slip Force
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Keep a force card on top while dealing a pile the spectator stops anywhere in.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. The force card starts on top of the deck.
  2. Deal cards face-down one at a time, inviting the spectator to say "stop" whenever they like.
  3. On every deal, push the top card forward with the thumb as if to take it, then slip it back flush and take the next card instead. The top stock never leaves the deck; every deal looks identical.
  4. On "stop," deal the top card honestly — the force card.
* **Pro Tip:** Practice the slip until the rhythm of a slipped deal and an honest deal are indistinguishable — record yourself from the front to check.


### 38. Spelling Bee
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Deal a 13-card packet so each card arrives exactly on the last letter of its spelled name.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Pre-arrange 13 cards: 3-8-7-A-Q-6-4-2-J-K-10-9-5. Memorize with the story: "387 years ago, a queen, 64 years old, had 2 sons, Jack and King. Jack worked until 10, but King worked a 9 to 5."
  2. Hold the packet face-down; an opening false shuffle sells that it is mixed.
  3. For each rank ace through king, spell its name aloud, moving one card from top to bottom per letter; after the last letter, the next card on top is the named card — set it aside.
* **Pro Tip:** Spell at a natural talking pace. Rushing the spelling is what makes it look like a procedure instead of magic.

### 39. Lazy Magician
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** The spectator finds their own card by following your instructions; you never touch the deck.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. They shuffle, then peek at the top card behind your back.
  2. They move cards from the bottom to the top equal to their card's value (J=11, Q=12, K=13).
  3. Take the deck and deal one card at a time, counting 1, 2, 3… starting from the second card dealt.
  4. The first card whose value matches your count is theirs — it started on top and N cards went above it, so it sits exactly N+1 down.
* **Pro Tip:** In Koran's presentation they do everything including the reveal. Stand back, stay quiet, and let the impossibility land on them.

### 40. Poker Player's Picnic
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** The spectator cuts four piles themselves; an ace tops each one.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Secretly stack the four aces on top of the deck.
  2. They cut the deck into four piles by lifting off packets and laying them in a row.
  3. Every cut takes cards off the top, so an ace rides along as the top card of each new pile.
  4. Turn the top card of each pile over one by one.
* **Pro Tip:** Have them make the last cut small — a tall fourth pile with an ace on top reads better than four even ones.

### 61. Kruskal Count
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** A spectator selects a secret word and follows a numerical path through a face-down layout to land on a predicted target card.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Deal a block of cards face-up into a row, letting the spectator mentally choose a starting point or word length rule (e.g., face cards = 5, numbers = face value, aces = 1).
  2. Follow the numerical value of the first card to jump forward to the next card, continuing the chain until reaching a final card.
  3. Open a pre-written prediction to show you knew exactly where their numerical path would terminate.
* **Pro Tip:** The mathematical convergence built into Kruskal's principle makes this foolproof as long as the counting rules are clear.

### 62. 8-Card Brainwave (Posterior Total)
* **Difficulty:** Beginner / Intermediate
* **Objective:** A spectator thinks of a number, names a suit, and finds that their mentally selected card is the only face-up card in a small packet.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Start with a pocket packet of 8 specially oriented cards.
  2. Ask the spectator to choose a number between 1 and 8 and a suit.
  3. Count down to their chosen number using a specialized counting sequence (such as the Gilbreath principle).
  4. Spread the packet to reveal that their named card is facing the opposite direction of every other card.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep your patter focused entirely on their freedom of choice to maximize the psychological impact of the self-working math.

### 63. Spectator Cuts to the Aces
* **Difficulty:** Beginner (Self-Working)
* **Objective:** The spectator cuts the deck into four piles; the top card of each pile is an ace.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Start with the aces on top (or preserved through a false shuffle).
  2. Guide the spectator to cut into four roughly equal piles — their actions should feel completely free.
  3. A simple policy (balancing packets or one covert top-card adjustment) places an ace on each pile.
  4. Turn over the four tops: four aces.
* **Pro Tip:** Many versions exist (Vernon and standard dealers' school among them). Pick one method and sell the spectator's freedom hard.
* **On the site:** Card t74, "Spectator Cuts to the Aces."

### 64. Piano Card Trick
* **Difficulty:** Beginner (Self-Working)

* **Mechanics:**

  2. Spectator isolates a pair (or indicates which holds their thought-of card).
  3. Remaining pairs eliminated or kept by a simple rule.
  4. The mate of their card is forced into the open.
* **Pro Tip:** Sell the freedom of their choice. The method is automatic; the presentation is the trick.
* **On the site:** Card t78, "Piano Card Trick."

### 65. Any Card at Any Number

## Part 5: Flourishes

### 34. Thumb Fan
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Spread the deck into a clean one-handed semicircle display.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold the deck face-down in one hand, beveled slightly to the side.
  2. Press the opposite thumb onto the top edge and sweep it in a smooth, steady arc; the cards pivot around the holding hand's middle finger.
  3. Keep pressure even — too hard clumps cards, too light collapses the fan. Learn with a fresh deck; worn cards fight you.
  4. Close the fan one-handed as the natural finish.
* **Pro Tip:** A light dusting of fanning powder (or just a new deck) makes the first week dramatically easier.

### 35. The Dribble
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Cascade the cards hand to hand in a steady waterfall.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold the deck up in one hand, angled slightly downward, thumb along the top edge.
  2. Ease off thumb pressure just enough that cards escape one by one into the waiting hand below.
  3. Keep pressure constant and light — squeezing gives bursts, relaxing gives clumps.
* **Pro Tip:** Practice over a bed or couch. You'll drop plenty while calibrating, and chasing cards across the room kills practice momentum.

### 36. The Spring
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Shoot the deck in an arcing stream from hand to hand.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Grip the deck with thumb along one long edge, fingers along the other; bend the packet into a slight arch so the cards sit under tension.
  2. Aim at the waiting hand and let the thumb release gradually — the cards fly in a tight stream.
  3. Start with hands close together; add distance only once the release is smooth. Relaxed, steady pressure out-throws muscling.
* **Pro Tip:** Learn the dribble first — it's the same controlled release pointed downward, and it calibrates the exact thumb pressure the spring needs.

### 37. Sybil Cut
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Perform a five-packet false cut that looks like thorough mixing but preserves deck order.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Split the deck into four or five packets.
  2. Pick up, display, twirl, and set down the packets in the choreographed sequence (taught step-by-step in the linked tutorial) — every packet lands back exactly where it started.
  3. The deck order never changes; the mixing is pure theater.
* **Pro Tip:** Learn it packet by packet at half speed. Speed is the last thing you add — the sequence has to live in your hands before it can look effortless.

### 41. Gemini Twins
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Two predictions set aside match cards a spectator freely stops at.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Borrow a shuffled deck. Fan through to remove two "prediction" cards, secretly noting the top and bottom cards — the predictions you pull are their mates.
  2. They deal into a pile and stop anywhere; lay the first prediction face-up on the pile and drop the deck on top, sandwiching it directly under the original bottom card.
  3. Repeat with the second prediction — it lands directly under the original top card.
  4. Spread: each face-up prediction sits beside its mate. Lift each pair to show the matches.
* **Pro Tip:** Don't rush the spreads. The audience needs to see the predictions sitting next to strangers before the pairs are lifted.

### 42. Impromptu Invisible Deck
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A freely named card is the only face-up card in the deck.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. They name any card — genuinely free, no force.
  2. During an innocent spread-square-cut sequence, locate the named card and reverse it under the deck, typically with a finger break above it as you square up.
  3. Spread the deck face-down: 51 backs, one face-up card — the named one.
* **Pro Tip:** Name the card out loud yourself as you spread ("…looking for the seven of clubs…") so the reversal happens under conversational cover.

### 43. Three-Card Monte
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Run the classic street monte as entertainment: the money card is never where they swear it is.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Three cards — two losers, one money card (usually a queen). Toss them face-down in a row.
  2. The "hype": holding two cards as one, appear to throw the bottom card while actually throwing the top, keeping the money card's position controlled.
  3. A bent corner on the money card is the convincer they track.
  4. Secretly transfer the bend to a loser mid-routine; they pick wrong with total confidence.
* **Pro Tip:** Present it as a demonstration of a famous scam, never as a game. The history is the entertainment; the betting is the crime.

### 44. Card to Ceiling
* **Difficulty:** Beginner (plus supplies)
* **Objective:** A signed card thrown at the ceiling sticks there.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Under natural handling, press a small pellet of magician's wax onto the back of the signed card.
  2. Throw it spinning toward the ceiling, flat like a frisbee, back-first.
  3. The wax grabs on impact. Point it out overhead, or let it drop later for the reveal.
* **Pro Tip:** Test the wax and the ceiling before anyone is watching. A card that flutters down mid-trick is a lesson you only need once. And get permission — not every ceiling qualifies.

### 45. McDonald's Aces
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate (plus gaffs)
* **Objective:** Four aces vanish from under cover cards and gather in a spectator's hands.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Display the four aces; cover three each with three indifferent cards; a spectator holds the fourth packet.
  2. The aces vanish from the three tabled packets one by one — each spread shows only indifferent cards.
  3. The spectator's packet is spread: all four aces.
  4. The method uses double-faced cards — each displayed "ace" is flipped or exchanged during the covering so the packet is really all indifferent cards.
* **Pro Tip:** This is the only gaffed routine on the page. Treat the double-facers casually — the more ordinary your handling, the less anyone suspects the cards themselves.

### 63. The Cascade Shuffle (Riffle Shuffle & Cascade)
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Visibly interweave two halves of a deck together smoothly from the bottom corners up.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Square the deck into two equal halves. Angle the inner corners of the packets together.
  2. Lift the inner corners slightly with your thumbs while pushing the packets into one another; the cards should spring and weave alternative corners automatically.
  3. Drop the top thumbs and let the interlaced bottom corners cascade into each other by releasing downward pressure.
  4. Square the interwoven block by pushing the sides together or bridging the deck to flatten it.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep your touch light; forcing the cards together too aggressively bends corners and ruins the smooth waterfall effect.

### 64. One-Handed Fan
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** Open the deck into a complete circular or semi-circular fan using only a single hand.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold the deck from above with the thumb at the inner edge and fingers resting across the top face.
  2. Plant the thumb firmly near the bottom-left corner and pivot the top cards outward in a sweeping circular motion using the index and middle fingers as guides.
  3. Distribute pressure evenly across the outer edge until a full 180-degree fan locks into place.
* **Pro Tip:** This flourish relies entirely on thumb traction and finger flexibility; practice with a broken-in, clean deck rather than a brand-new, slippery pack.

### 65. Hot Shot Cut
* **Difficulty:** Advanced
* **Objective:** A one-handed cut that shoots a card or small packet out of the deck.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Deck in a one-hand grip with tension built into the packet.
  2. A controlled snap sends one card (or thin packet) flying forward.
  3. Catch it or let it land face-up as a reveal.
* **Pro Tip:** Start close to the table so misses are quiet. Distance comes after accuracy.
* **On the site:** Card t80, "Hot Shot Cut."
