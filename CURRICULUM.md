# Card Tricks Curriculum

A progressive syllabus for the thirteen tricks in the [Sleight School guide](https://scomofo.github.io/card-tricks/). Foundations first, moves second, routines last — each part builds on the one before it.

---

## Part 1: Foundations

### 1. Key Card Principle
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Secretly track a spectator's card by noting the identity of the card immediately adjacent to it.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Casually note or glimpse the bottom card of the deck before starting (your key card).
  2. Have a card selected, shown to the audience, and returned to the top of the deck.
  3. Cut the deck once, genuinely, about halfway. The cut brings your key card directly on top of the selection. (Do not riffle shuffle here — a genuine shuffle would destroy the setup.)
  4. Spread through the face-up cards. Locate your key card; the spectator's selection is the card immediately below it.
* **Pro Tip:** Never look for the selection directly. Scan smoothly for your key card so your gaze doesn't betray the search.

### 2. Overhand Shuffle Control
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Keep a chosen card exactly where you want it while the shuffle looks honest.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. **To send a card to the bottom:** it starts on top. Shuffle overhand normally — peeling small packets off the top of the right-hand packet into the left hand. The first card peeled (theirs) ends up on the bottom of the reassembled deck. One honest overhand shuffle moves the top card to the bottom.
  2. **To keep a bottom card there:** on later shuffles, leave the bottom stock sitting in your left hand. Lift packets only from the top, peel, and drop them back on top. The bottom cards never move.
  3. **The injog:** to bookmark a position, let the final packet land protruding about a centimeter toward you instead of squared. Cut to that step whenever you need the spot.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the rhythm identical whether you're controlling cards or genuinely mixing them.

### 3. Hindu Shuffle & Force
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Force a predetermined card on a spectator using the rhythm of a Hindu shuffle.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Position your force card on the *top* of the deck.
  2. Hold the deck face-down in the left hand. The right hand comes over the top and pulls the top portion away to the right.
  3. The left thumb strips small packets off the top of the right-hand packet back into the left hand, repeatedly. The right hand keeps hold of the bottom stub throughout.
  4. The bottom card of the right-hand packet never changes during the shuffle — it is always the original top card, your force card. Have the spectator say "stop" at any point, then tilt the right-hand packet to display its bottom card as their "free choice."
  5. Drop the stub on top to finish, holding a left pinky break where it lands so you can cut straight to the force card later.
* **Pro Tip:** Don't pause when they say stop — flow straight into turning the packet over. Hesitation is what makes a force look like a force.

### 4. Double Lift
* **Difficulty:** Beginner / Intermediate
* **Objective:** Lift two cards as a single card — the engine inside half of card magic.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. As you square the deck, hold a small break with the left pinky under the top two cards.
  2. The right hand takes the double from above by the ends — thumb at the inner end, fingers at the outer — lifting both cards as one.
  3. Turn it over like a book page to display the face, then turn it back the same way.
  4. The top card of the deck is now secretly the second card.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the two cards perfectly squared. A split edge is the only tell — practice the turnover in a mirror until a double feels identical to a single.

### 5. False Cut
* **Difficulty:** Beginner
* **Objective:** Appear to cut the deck while leaving the entire order undisturbed.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. With the deck in the right hand, the left hand swing-cuts the top half off.
  2. Complete the cut: place the right-hand half on top of the left-hand half. One genuine cut — the halves have swapped.
  3. Do the exact same thing a second time. Two identical half-cuts restore the original order completely.
* **Pro Tip:** Two cuts look more honest than one. Do it mid-sentence, casually, exactly as you would before any trick.

## Part 2: The Moves

### 1. The Glide
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Secretly deal the second-from-bottom card while appearing to deal the bottom card.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold the deck face-down in the left hand.
  2. The left fingertips pull the bottom card back about half an inch toward the palm, exposing the card directly above it.
  3. The right hand takes the exposed card — the second from the bottom — leaving the original bottom card concealed behind in the left hand.
* **Pro Tip:** Never tilt the face of the deck toward the audience while the bottom card is pulled back. The audience must believe they just saw the bottom card dealt.

### 2. Riffle Force
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Force a predetermined card while the spectator believes their "stop" chose it.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Place the force card on top of the deck.
  2. Hold the deck in the left hand and riffle down the outer edge with the right thumb. The spectator says "stop" whenever they like.
  3. Freeze at the break. The right hand lifts the upper packet at the stop point — and as it lifts, the left thumb secretly drags the top card back so it stays on the lower packet. Keep the motion small and covered by the right hand.
  4. Display the top card of the lower packet: "the card you stopped at."
* **Pro Tip:** Protect the spectator's belief that the stop chose the card. That belief does most of the work.

### 3. Elmsley Count
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** Count four cards aloud while one of them never shows its face.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold four cards face-down in the left hand.
  2. With the right hand, take the cards one at a time off the top, counting "1, 2, 3, 4."
  3. Cards 2 and 4 are taken *under* the right-hand packet instead of on top — the right hand dips slightly to receive each one beneath.
  4. The audience sees four cards counted; one card was hidden the entire time.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep the rhythm metronome-even. Rushing counts 2 and 4 is what exposes the dip — slow is smooth, and smooth is invisible.

### 4. Snap Change
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate / Advanced
* **Objective:** Visibly transform one face-up card into another with a snap of the fingers.
* **Mechanics:**
  1. Hold a small packet face-up in the left hand. The right hand comes over from above.
  2. Press the right middle and ring fingertips onto the face of the top card, near its top edge.
  3. In one sharp snapping motion, drag the top card back toward you — it flips face-down under cover of the right hand, revealing the card beneath. The snap sound plus the speed is what reads as a visual change.
  4. Commit fully to the motion; hesitation is the only thing that exposes it.
* **Pro Tip:** Play it to someone directly in front of you with the packet held low. It's devastating head-on and ordinary from the side.

## Part 3: Routines

### 1. 21-Card Trick
* **Difficulty:** Beginner (Self-Working)
* **Objective:** A mathematical classic — the spectator's card isolates itself at position 11 with zero sleight of hand.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Deal 21 cards face-up into three columns of seven, dealing across row by row.
  2. The spectator memorizes a card and tells you which column it's in.
  3. Gather the columns so their column ends up sandwiched *between* the other two.
  4. Repeat the deal-and-gather twice more — three rounds total, their column in the middle every time.
  5. Their card is now exactly 11th from the top. Count down and reveal it.
* **Pro Tip:** Each round narrows the card's possible position; three rounds leave exactly one. Keep casual conversation going while gathering so nobody tracks the pattern.

### 2. Two-Card Monte
* **Difficulty:** Beginner / Intermediate
* **Objective:** A pocket-sized impossibility — the spectator can never find the Ace, no matter how closely they watch.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. Show two cards, e.g. the Ace of Spades and the King of Hearts. "Watch the Ace."
  2. Turn the two-card packet face-down *as one block*, exactly like a double lift. The order never changes — that is the entire method.
  3. Do the "throw": toss or flip the packet between your hands with a flourish. It looks like a switch. It isn't.
  4. "Where's the Ace — top or bottom?" Whatever they answer, show them they're wrong.
  5. Repeat. The comedy is that they can never win — lean into it.
* **Pro Tip:** Nothing sneaky happened, yet the spectator is certain something did. Conviction beats complexity; sell the game, not the method.

### 3. Ambitious Card
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A signed card repeatedly rises from the middle of the deck back to the top.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. A card is selected, signed, and controlled to the top.
  2. Push it face-up into the middle of the deck. Snap your fingers — it's back on top.
  3. Show it with a double lift, turn the double face-down, then take the top card (secretly an indifferent card) and insert *that* into the middle. The spectator swears their card went in; it never left the top.
  4. Repeat with escalating impossibility — lose it for real, box the deck — until the finale.
* **Pro Tip:** Vary the control method in each phase so the trick can't be reverse-engineered. Two clean phases beat four shaky ones; add a phase a week and the routine grows with you.

### 4. Triumph
* **Difficulty:** Intermediate
* **Objective:** A deck shuffled face-up into face-down rights itself completely — except for the spectator's card.
* **Routine Flow:**
  1. A card is chosen, signed, and controlled to the top, placed face-up on the face-down deck.
  2. Protect that card's orientation while apparently mixing the deck: overhand shuffles that keep the top stock, false cuts — turning a few packets face-up as you go. The mixing is theater; the one orientation you protect is the trick.
  3. Square the deck slowly and spread it: every card face-down except theirs, face-up.
* **Pro Tip:** The classic Vernon handling uses a slip shuffle for the mixing phase. Whatever handling you choose, the shuffle must be false or controlled — a genuinely randomized deck can't be unmixed. The spectator watched the mess happen, which is why the cleanup lands so hard.
